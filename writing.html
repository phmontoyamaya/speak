The ABT (Olson)

[Context] AND [more context], BUT [the problem or gap], THEREFORE [what you will do].

The Dobzhansky template finds your one word: "Nothing in ______ makes sense except in the light of ______." The ABT is the sentence. A short hero's journey (ordinary world, problem, what you did, what you came back with) is the paragraph.

OCAR and LD-R (Schimel)

  • Opening: the problem space the reader already cares about.
  • Challenge: the specific gap or question. This is your "but".
  • Action: what you will do to answer it.
  • Resolution: what we will know or have when you finish.

LD-R (Lead, Development, Resolution) puts the punchline first. Use it where the reader is impatient: summaries, aims pages, review summaries.

Sentence checklist

  1. Does the sentence start with something the reader already knows?
  2. Does it end on the new or important information?
  3. Is the verb within the first 8–10 words?
  4. Is the action in the verb, not hidden in a -tion or -ment noun?
  5. Is it one idea? Over 35 words needs a reason.
  6. Is every "this" followed by a noun?
  7. Is each key term the same word every time?
  8. Does the verb match the strength of the evidence?

Review template

1. SUMMARY (2–3 sentences, your own words) What they propose, why, and how. 2. MAJOR STRENGTHS (tie each to a scoring criterion) 3. MAJOR CONCERNS (for each: what, where, why it matters, a fix) 4. MINOR POINTS (one line each) 5. OVERALL (one ABT: strengths AND fit, BUT main risk, THEREFORE score / recommendation)

Phrases that work: "The proposal would be stronger if…" · "It is unclear how…" · "I could not find…" · "This is a major concern because…" · "A minor point:" · "The applicants should clarify…" · "This aim is well justified by…"
